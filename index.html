Societies & Groups

The Athlone Pipers

A Century of Piping Tradition

From the Clan Uisneach Pipe Band in the late 19th century to the modern Athlone Town Pipe Band, piping has been a central thread in local identity.

Athlone Musical Society

Ireland’s Oldest Active Society

Co-founded by Henry Foy in 1902, this society is the longest continuously active musical group in Ireland, staging annual dramatic and musical productions.

Athlone Choral Society

Community Harmony

Performing classical masterpieces and contemporary arrangements, the choral society brings together community vocalists across Westmeath and Roscommon.

Comhaltas Ceoltóirí Éireann

Guardians of Irish Music

The Athlone branch has nurtured traditional music, song, and dance for generations, hosting major festivals including the 1953 Fleadh Cheoil na hÉireann.

Timeline of Musical & Radio History

1796

Patrick Keating is born. The Waterloo veteran will return to Athlone to spark a 19th-century musical band revival.

1820s

Keating forms and trains disciplined civic brass and reed bands in Athlone.

1843

Keating’s Athlone Band plays at Daniel O’Connell’s Monster Meeting at Summerhill.

1849

Young Patrick S. Gilmore emigrates to Boston after receiving musical training under Keating in Athlone.

1863

Gilmore publishes “When Johnny Comes Marching Home” during service in the American Civil War.

1870s

Michael Kilkelly leads the Athlone Brass Band at Land League demonstrations and evictions.

1890s

Kilkelly detects the gifted voice of young John McCormack and provides his earliest vocal training.

1902

Henry Foy co-founds Athlone Musical Society; John McCormack wins Gold at the Dublin Feis Ceoil.

1912

Athlone uilleann piper Eugene Daly survives the sinking of RMS Titanic after playing "Erin's Lament" on deck.

1919

Henry Foy establishes the Harry Foy Jazz Quartette — widely regarded as Ireland’s first jazz group.

1932

The high-power Athlone transmitter at Moydrum broadcasts John McCormack at the Eucharistic Congress nationwide.

6 Feb 1933

Éamon de Valera officially opens the Athlone station, hailing it as "Droichead Nua Átha Luain" (A new bridge of Athlone).

1953

Athlone hosts the Fleadh Cheoil na hÉireann, celebrating traditional Irish music nationwide.

Radio Heritage

Athlone sits at the center of Irish broadcasting history. The high-power transmitter at Moydrum gave the young state its voice across Europe and the Irish diaspora.

The High-Power Transmitting Station, Moydrum

Built to give Ireland nationwide and European broadcasting coverage, the Athlone station entered temporary service for the 1932 Eucharistic Congress and was formally launched on 6 February 1933 by Éamon de Valera.

He famously described the station as “a new bridge of Athlone” spanning the seas to link Ireland with its international diaspora. Equipping a Marconi 60 kW transmitter with dual guyed masts, the dial marking "Athlone" became standard on valve radios throughout Europe.

2RN to Radio Éireann

1926 – 1930s

From early low-power broadcasts in Dublin to the full high-power launch at Athlone, medium wave 531 kHz made national programming accessible across all 32 counties.

McCormack & Eucharistic Congress

1932 Milestone

John McCormack’s legendary performance of 'Panis Angelicus' was transmitted nationwide via Athlone, uniting millions in a single historic listening moment.

Preserving Moydrum Station

Industrial Heritage

The surviving Marconi transmitter building and vintage valve apparatus at Moydrum offer a world-class foundation for Ireland's national broadcasting museum.

About the Athlone Music Heritage Project

The Athlone Music Heritage Project is a community initiative founded by local historians, musicologists, and broadcasting enthusiasts. Our mission is to preserve, interpret, and celebrate Athlone's musical and radio history.

Content has been researched through local archives, contemporary newspaper files, and contributions from families across Ireland and the diaspora.
