Two Centuries of Sound & Wireless Telegraphy

1796 & 1820s

Patrick Keating is born. Following service at Waterloo, he returns to Athlone to institute military band training, igniting an 80-year midlands band revival.

Bands & Revival
1843 – 1849

Keating's Athlone band headlines Daniel O’Connell’s Monster Meeting at Summerhill. Keating trains young Patrick Gilmore before Gilmore departs for Boston in 1849.

Bands & Revival
1863 – 1869

Gilmore publishes “When Johnny Comes Marching Home” during the US Civil War and establishes the monumental Boston Peace Jubilee with 1,000 players and 10,000 choristers.

Bands & Revival
Late 1870s

Michael Kilkelly leads the Athlone Brass Band at Land War evictions, transforming municipal brass music into a weapon of agrarian civil resistance.

Bands & Revival
11 May 1898

In nearby Clara, Co. Offaly, Guglielmo Marconi establishes a link between Goodbody’s Charlestown Mill and Clashawaun Jute Works: Ireland's first wireless test and the world's first commercial wireless message.

Marconi & Transmitters
1902 & 1919

Henry Foy co-founds Athlone Musical Society. In 1919, he forms the Harry Foy Jazz Quartette — widely acknowledged as Ireland's first jazz ensemble.

Jazz & Resistance
15 April 1912

Athlone uilleann piper Eugene Daly survives the RMS Titanic sinking after piping on deck and rescuing fellow Athlone passenger Bertha Mulvihill.

Pipes & Maritime
June 1932

The newly constructed Athlone (Moydrum) Marconi transmitter is powered up to relay Count John McCormack singing Panis Angelicus at the 31st International Eucharistic Congress.

Marconi & Transmitters
6 February 1933

Éamon de Valera officially inaugurates the Athlone Transmitting Station (567 kHz / 530m) as "Droichead nua Átha Luain" — a bridge connecting Ireland with its diaspora.

Marconi & Transmitters
1934 – 1935

Former Athlone curate Fr Peter Conefrey leads the Gaelic League Anti-Jazz march in Mohill; radio and gramophone jazz imports result in the 1935 Public Dance Halls Act.

Midlands Heritage Route

Explore the Athlone & Marconi Clara Trail

A self-guided historic pilgrimage connecting Athlone's urban musical landmarks with the Marconi industrial innovation sites across the Offaly border.

Stop 01 • The Bawn, Irishtown

John McCormack's Birthplace

A commemorative bronze plaque marks where the legendary tenor was born on 14 June 1884. Kilkelly discovered McCormack singing in the Marist College choir just streets away.

Stop 02 • Chapel Street

Dean Crowe Theatre & Arts Centre

Historic rehearsal hall and home of the Athlone Musical Society (founded 1902 by Henry Foy), continuously staging operatic and choral productions for over 120 years.

Stop 03 • St. Mary's Place

Site of The Crescent Ballroom

The epicenter of the Irish Showband boom of the 1950s and 60s, hosting legendary national showbands and drawing crowds across the Midlands.

Stop 04 • Moydrum (567 kHz)

Moydrum Transmitting Station

Ireland’s historic high-power broadcasting station. The surviving Marconi transmitter halls and two 100m mast footings represent one of Europe's foremost early broadcasting monuments.

Stop 05 • Clara, Co. Offaly (20 Mins East)

Marconi Dickensian Mile & Goodbody Mills

The historic 1898 spark-gap transmission path between the Charlestown Flour Mill and Clashawaun Jute Works, funded by the Quaker Goodbody family.

Strategic Plan

The Permanent Museum Vision

Preserving Athlone's dual legacy of performance and wireless technology in a world-class cultural institution.

I

Secure & Archive

Gathering 19th-century band instruments, rare recordings, and surviving Moydrum valve transmitters into a protected civic repository.

II

Immersive Sound

Creating interactive vintage valve studios and acoustic listening chambers illustrating Athlone's role as Ireland’s voice to the diaspora.

III

Education

Supporting radio documentaries, sound workshops, and youth traditional and jazz academies in collaboration with regional schools.

IV

Heritage Tourism

Linking Athlone Castle, the Old Rail Trail Greenway, and Clara's Marconi heritage into a unified cultural corridor.
